# Patrickpoix

**Financial Mathematics · Quant Research · AI Agent Systems · Financial Data Engineering**

I am a Financial Mathematics undergraduate focused on building research and engineering systems at the intersection of **finance, data and AI**. My work spans buy-side/sell-side research, point-in-time quantitative research infrastructure, cross-market portfolio analytics and long-horizon AI Agent harnesses.

I care less about demos that only work once and more about systems that remain **auditable, reproducible and explicit about failure**: point-in-time data boundaries, immutable research evidence, bounded experiments, human authority for high-impact actions, and tests that distinguish engineering correctness from research validity.

## Selected engineering scale

- **SA Research OS:** private development corpus covered a 543-symbol CN research universe, 22,597 official disclosure PDFs → 90,388 structured fact rows; US research used a 17-year window, 3,349 securities and approximately 10 million point-in-time rows.
- **Longbridge Research OS:** private development corpus reached 424 immutable Runs / 97 Editions / ~2.08 GB; one history-list I/O redesign reduced first-screen latency from roughly 9.8 seconds to sub-second on that corpus.
- **Pi-One:** public review snapshot includes a reproducible offline build, 93 Harness tests and a 122-test public evaluation surface; private experimental datasets remain outside the public repository.

The scale figures above describe development/evaluation corpora and engineering workloads. They are not investment-performance claims.

## Research & engineering approach

```text
financial question
      ↓
point-in-time / provenance contract
      ↓
data + model / Agent workflow
      ↓
bounded experiment & evaluation
      ↓
negative-result retention + failure attribution
      ↓
human-reviewed promotion
```

My recurring themes are **Python / SQL quantitative research**, **TypeScript/Node systems**, AI Agent orchestration and evaluation, immutable evidence, failure analysis, and turning financial-domain requirements into systems that can be independently checked.
